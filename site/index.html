Built on the habit loop.
The habit just isn't yours anymore.

Nir Eyal's Hooked describes how products capture people: trigger, action, variable reward, investment. Job portals run that loop on you every night. Dispatch takes the same four stages and hands the compulsive part to a machine — you keep the rewards.

01 · Trigger

The dread was the trigger. Now it's the feed.

The old internal trigger: that low-grade guilt at 11pm that you should be applying. Dispatch replaces it with an external one — it discovers new postings on your feed and queues them itself. Opportunity pings you. Obligation doesn't.

the old trigger

the old trigger

the old trigger

The silence after submit. Did it even go through? No receipt, no record, no idea.

02 · Action

One action: arm a session.

The simplest behavior wins. Yours is one click with hard bounds you set — a timer and caps. Inside that window the machine discovers, fills, verifies, and submits. Outside it, nothing moves. Restart the console and it's disarmed. Always.

03 · Variable reward

Wake up to a report, not a to-do list.

Every session ends differently — that's the point. Verified submissions with receipts. Outreach drafts to real people at the company, waiting in your mailbox. Walls parked with the evidence attached. You open the console to find out what happened, not to do it.

the hunt

Verified submissions — screenshot, receipt, confirmation text. Never "probably went through".

the tribe

Outreach drafts to alumni and engineers at each company — generated, validated, never sent without you.

the self

A cleared queue. Walls that needed a human are parked with their evidence; everything else moved.

04 · Investment

Every answer you give, you never give again.

Stored value compounds. Each thing you feed Dispatch makes the next session need less of you — and overnight discovery reloads the queue, so the loop re-arms itself.

Your profile, once→ every form, every portal
An essay answer you wrote→ banked for every board that asks again
Your resumes, labeled by domain→ auto-attached per application
A persona for outreach→ drafts that sound like you
Every fill outcome→ selectors that learn which fields flake

Autonomy you arm.
Not autonomy you hope for.

The reason you can sleep while it runs isn't trust — it's architecture. Every claim below is enforced in code, not in a promise.

Bounded by construction

A session is a timer and two caps backed by an atomic counter. A crash can't reset it; disarming makes further submits structurally impossible.

Every gate still runs

Arming removes exactly one thing: the per-application "are you sure". Field verification, upload verification, page identity, the approved plan — all still gate every click.

Walls park, with evidence

CAPTCHA, logins, essay questions — anything that needs a human parks the application with a review item and its evidence. Nothing is force-clicked.

Your words stay yours

Essays and self-ID questions are never auto-answered. Outreach stops at drafts in your own mailbox — the codebase can't send mail, and CI proves it.

Put one operator confidently in command of many applications — every submission deliberate, every state honest, every outcome accounted for.
